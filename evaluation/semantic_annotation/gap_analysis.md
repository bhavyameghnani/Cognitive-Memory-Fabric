# Gap analysis: which semantic rules to fix first

Evidence: `diagnostics.json` and `known_defects.md` (every case reproduced from the stored database; 37 of 37). These are rule-level observations; real accuracy is unknown until the sample is annotated. **No rule has been changed.** Priority = risk to a future memory-formation phase x how mechanical the fix is.

| # | Gap | Evidence | Why it ranks here | Direction (not implemented) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `corrects` links and the correction cue | Date check on the 17 system `corrects` links: 3 agree with the dated earlier email, 4 disagree, 1 ambiguous (EML-022: two same-day messages), 9 name no date. 7 of 21 `correction` labels fire only on an apology ("Sorry, I'm squeezing this..."), and 5 of the 17 links come from it. | A wrong correction target silently supersedes the wrong fact. Highest harm, and the evidence for a target (an explicit date or quoted content) is available in the text. | Require explicit reference evidence for a target; resolve it by the date/medium the text names; emit no link otherwise (`unresolved`). Remove the apology cue. |
| 2 | Decision attribution | 75 of 97 `decision` labels are on advisor turns; EML-001 labelled `decision` was later corrected as overstating what the client said (EML-028). The label carries no maker, status or attribution. | Promoting an advisor recap to a client decision is the central governance risk for memory. | Add maker/status/attribution fields; advisor-spoken recaps become `reported_by_other`; first-hand client turns are the only `confirmed` source. |
| 3 | Dialogue-act false positives | 84 of 453 `agreement` labels start with "Good morning/Good question/Right now"; 24 of 84 `rejection` labels are preference statements and 2 are courtesy "No ..."; "I'll have to think about that" is `commitment`; 2 "the decision is yours" turns are `decision`; 77 `question` labels hang on one mid-turn '?'; 40 turns carry 3+ acts. | Mechanical, cheap, and they inflate every downstream count. | Anchor openers and require the cue to start the clause; add `preference`; stop multi-labelling agenda openers. |
| 4 | Missing acts | No `request`, `preference`, `exploration` or `answer` act; a question without '?' is missed; "Nothing is decided" is not a deferral. 1,038 of 2,548 turns carry only the default `information`. | Requests and preferences are common memory-relevant content. | Add the acts to the label set (they already exist in the gold schema). |
| 5 | Topic cues | `rent` matches "current/different/parental" in 109 of 111 turns; `son ` matches "comparison/reason" (23 of 23); modal `will` makes 32 of 63 `estate_and_trusts` segments; `portal` makes 120 turns `scheduling_and_admin`; `plan ` 115 turns; 253 of 681 segments have confidence of 0.4 or lower and 11 are `general_discussion`. | Topic is the index for retrieval; wrong topics make correct evidence unfindable. | Whole-word matching, remove modal/function words, require two independent cues or a phrase for broad topics. |
| 6 | Temporal and amount extraction | 19 of 27 "May" hits are the modal verb; amount values lose their unit ("$1.46 m" for "$1.46 million", 70 turns). | Mechanical regex fixes; temporal anchors matter for currency of a fact. | Case-sensitive month names; order the alternation; keep the whole matched span. |
| 7 | `answers` relation and segmentation | 143 of 692 `answers` links cross a segment boundary; 13 answer a multi-question turn; 33 single-turn and 33 mixed-topic segments; 8 of 22 email threads and 4 of 28 chats are split. | Context retrieval returns half an exchange. Needs gold boundaries first, so it is last. | Segment first, then link within a segment; keep a question and its adjacent answer together; avoid splitting a short thread. |

Order of work: (1) and (2) first because they affect what could become a stored fact; (3), (5) and (6) are mechanical and can be done together; (4) and (7) need the annotation results to set the targets.
